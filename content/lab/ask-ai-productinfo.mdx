---
title: 'Internal Product Documentation Chatbot'
slug: 'ask-ai-productinfo'
problem: >
  Product knowledge (installation, configuration, troubleshooting,
  roadmap) lives scattered across Confluence, exported PDFs, and the
  heads of a few people. Support , Sales and CS, the people who need it
  most, don't have access to those tools or a fast way to find
  answers. The same questions get answered by hand, repeatedly, over
  Slack and email.
decision: >
  Build a focused internal Q&A tool rather than another documentation
  interface: users select a product, and the chatbot searches only
  that product's documentation and conversation history.

  I kept the prototype lightweight — Python, FastAPI and SQLite —
  because the real corpus was only 857 chunks. I also measured model
  cost and latency rather than assuming the largest model was necessary:
  switching to a smaller model cut cost by 3x and median latency from
  5.60s to 3.24s, with groundedness intact and a one-point completeness
  trade-off.
rigor: >
  Four findings changed the implementation:

  (1) Missing context, not missing content. "Install" appeared in 4/42
  chunks using text alone, versus 28/42 when the heading path was
  included. Adding the heading path improved installation retrieval
  from 1/10 to 9/10 and an early evaluation from 19/23 to 23/23.

  (2) Retrieval depth was the bottleneck. Two mechanisms built around
  a presumed missing-information problem fixed only 1 of 3 missed facts.
  Increasing K from 10 to 50 showed the missing facts at ranks 11–13.
  The problem was retrieval depth, not missing knowledge.

  (3) The UI hid a data-loss bug. Syncing one documentation branch
  silently removed previous branches for the same product. Changing
  replacement scope from product-level to branch-level fixed it.

  (4) The evaluation had to measure regressions as well as improvements.
  A separate 21-case harness moved from 85.7% to 100% after increasing K,
  dropped to 95.2% with a cheaper generation model, then recovered to
  100% after tightening completeness rules.
traction: >
  Shown to several people in Support, Product and CS internally; positive
  reaction feedback. Now deployed to stage environment and people actively using it.
nextVersion: >
  Expand the evaluation set beyond the initial document and establish
  a repeatable evaluation suite before making further retrieval or
  generation changes. The next iteration will focus on broader content
  coverage, retrieval edge cases and repeatability rather than adding
  more mechanisms, including image handling.
---

This started inside a hospitality SaaS platform, where product
knowledge, installation, configuration, troubleshooting, the
roadmap, was scattered across Confluence, exported PDFs, and the
heads of a handful of people. Support and CS, who needed it most,
didn't have access to fast way to search them. The
same questions kept getting answered by hand, over Slack and email.

Company and product names are omitted throughout — the underlying
documentation spans several internal products, and nothing below
depends on which ones.
